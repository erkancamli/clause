What it will not do

This pays on a measurement, not on your actual loss. If the ground shakes hard at your house but the nearest station records less, you are not paid. That gap is real, it is called basis risk, and being onchain does not close it. What we do instead is show you the index, the source and the odds before you pay, and let you move the radius until the cover matches the risk you actually have.

If the source publishes nothing at all by the deadline, the policy is unwound and your premium is returned in full. Not a payout, not a lapse. The contract could not be judged, so it does not stand.

What is real here, and what is simulated

Real

  • The pricing mathematics, written out by hand rather than imported, so every number can be traced
  • The hazard models: truncated Gutenberg and Richter with a characteristic fault term, gamma rainfall, lognormal delay
  • The capital standard, the pool accounting and a ledger that replays and reconciles
  • The price you are quoted on the previous page, computed in your browser

Simulated

  • The chain: Rialo shaped, 50 ms blocks, with a world clock running about 40,000 times real time
  • Readings, generated by the hazard models rather than fetched from live APIs
  • Station normals, route statistics and zone seismicity: plausible figures of the right shape, not a hazard assessment
  • The RPC client speaks the published Rialo methods and is what a devnet deployment would use

Solvency standard

Three tranches, one order of loss.

Capital is held in three layers. A loss is taken from the first loss tranche until it is gone, then from the mezzanine, and only then from the senior. The part of each premium that pays for expected loss goes to the layer expected to pay it; the margin is shared by capital times a weight, so the layer hit first earns the most for standing there.

The rules that bind it

SOLVENCY

Survive the worst half percent

Capital is sized as the average loss across the worst 0.5% of twenty thousand simulated years.

CONCENTRATION

No single event over 35%

A pool can be solvent on average and one earthquake away from nothing.

LOCKED

Capital cannot run

A provider may withdraw only what is not standing behind live policies.

AUDITED

Privacy

What is kept, and by whom

This page describes the Clause site and protocol as they run today. It is a plain account, not a contract.

Signing in with a key on this device

A key is made in your browser and its private half never leaves it. The protocol receives the public half, which is your address. No name, email, phone number or password is asked for or kept.

Signing in through Privy

If you choose email, Google, a passkey or a wallet, the sign in is run by Privy, a separate company. Privy keeps what you signed in with, such as your email address, under its own privacy policy. The protocol receives only a token naming your Privy account, checks it, and records which address that account acts as. It never receives your email.

Buying cover

A policy records its terms, the place or flight it covers, the value you declared as at risk and the address that holds it. Proofs of residency or ownership are checked and not stored: the protocol keeps one answer per claim, yes or no.

Payouts to a bank or a mobile wallet

If you choose to be paid at a bank or on a mobile wallet, the protocol keeps the IBAN and the name on the account, or the provider and phone number, because a payment cannot be made without them. They are shown back to you masked, and in full only to whoever makes the transfer: the issuing company, a licensed payment partner or, until one is connected, the operator.

Rider's permission to buy

If you let Rider buy cover for you, the protocol keeps the limits you signed and a fingerprint of the permission; the permission itself stays in this browser. Your conversations with Rider are not stored.

Your location and the map

When you are asked where the thing you insure is, the browser asks whether to share your location. If you allow it, the coordinates are sent to this protocol's server, which asks OpenStreetMap's Nominatim service what place they fall in; addresses you type are looked up the same way through Photon. Neither service is told who you are. The map tiles are loaded from OpenStreetMap, which sees your IP address as any website you visit does. Nothing about your location is kept except the point you choose to insure.

The contract and the insurer's checks

The contract you accept is kept with your application and your policy, and its hash is on the policy token. The address of a home or where a car is kept is shown to the company that issues the policy, so it can check that it is yours; the protocol keeps a fingerprint of it, not the address, on the policy record. Weather on the home page comes from Open-Meteo and is shown, not used to settle anything.

Companies

An insurer or reinsurer that registers gives its name, licence details and a contact address. These are shown to the reviewer who checks them and, once approved, the name and licence are public.

What this site stores in your browser

Your key, your language, your theme and the country you chose for the live bands. Nothing is used for advertising and no tracking scripts are loaded. The typefaces come from Google Fonts, so Google sees the request for them, as it would for any site that uses them.

You can remove your key from this browser at any time with "Forget this device" on the My cover page.

Questions

Questions people ask, and what the messages mean

Short answers, in plain words. Where a message on the page is quoted, it is quoted exactly, so you can find it here.

Buying cover

What is parametric cover?

It pays on a measurement, not on an assessment of your damage: an earthquake's intensity at your address, the wind at your place, the rain at the station, a flight's delay. When the measurement crosses the line written in the policy, the payout is made on its own. There is no claim to file and nobody to convince.

What is basis risk, and why does the page keep saying it?

The measurement can miss your loss both ways: a roof can go on a day short of the trigger, and a payout can arrive for a day that did no harm. That gap is basis risk. It is stated before you pay because a cover that hides it is not honest about what it is.

Who actually insures me?

A licensed company, chosen on the confirm step from those licensed where the thing you insure stands; its name, its supervisor and its fee are shown. The pool stands behind that company as capital. On this demonstration the companies are stand ins, marked as such, and nothing written against them is cover.

Why does it ask what I am protecting and whether it is inside the area?

Because that is the line between insurance and a wager. Cover is capped by what you say is at risk, and refused if the thing insured is not where the index is measured. Both are checked here and again by the protocol.

How is the price worked out?

Expected loss from the model, plus a load for the capital the risk ties up or for passing it to a reinsurer, plus expenses. The confirm step's "show how this price was worked out" lists each part and the chance of a payout.

Is this instead of DASK?

No. In Türkiye DASK is compulsory for dwellings and pays assessed damage up to its cap; this cover pays on the measurement, fast, and sits alongside it. The confirm step shows what the same home would pay DASK, for comparison.

Price and money

What is USDC, and where does the money on the demonstration come from?

USDC is the protocol's unit of account. On this demonstration new addresses are funded with demonstration money and there is a top up button on My cover; none of it is real. Where the law requires it, premiums and payouts settle in the local currency through the issuing company.

Can I pay in instalments?

Where your country's rules allow it, the confirm step offers plans. The first payment is held when you apply and taken when the company approves; a payment missed for more than fifteen days ends the policy, and anything still owed is deducted from a payout.

How do refunds and cancellation work?

Within an hour of buying, all of it comes back. Within the cooling off period your law gives, all of it comes back. After that, cover that cannot be foreseen (earthquake, storm) returns the unexpired part when you cancel; rain and flights run to the end of their term. Nothing can be cancelled once its trigger has been reached.

Does the platform charge me anything?

No. The price you see is the pool's price plus the issuing company's fee. The platform is paid by the company, out of that fee, and a fee that comes back to you takes the platform's share back with it.

After buying

Where is my policy?

On My cover, with its terms, its status and its token. The token is locked to your address and cannot be transferred; a cancelled policy's token is burned and leaves the wallet.

How is the reading decided, and what if the source is silent?

By the calculation rules, written before anything happens and printed as section 10 of your contract: which source is read and which stands in, what counts as the final figure and when it is frozen, the tests a reading must pass, the chain's attestation rule, and the objection window. The Calculation rules page shows them peril by peril.

Is my policy on a public chain?

The protocol's own chain is the record. Beside it, a public chain (Base Sepolia) is told of each token minted or burned, each settlement, and the root of the whole book on a timer. The Proof page shows what was sent and lets you check your policy's place in the book in your own browser.

How will I know it paid?

Settlement happens on chain the moment the reading crosses the trigger; My cover shows the payout and the Proof page shows the reading, the block and the policy it settled. A bank payout, where you chose one, follows within the days the company states.

Can I renew?

Yes. From My cover a policy that is ending can be renewed on the same terms, and a standing instruction can renew it on the day without you. The issuing company also sees which policies end within thirty days.

Applications and documents

Why is my purchase "waiting for the insurer"?

A home or a vehicle is applied for, not bought on the spot: the issuing company screens you against sanctions lists, confirms who you are above a certain size, and confirms the thing is yours. Your premium is held, not taken. Approved, the policy is written at the price you agreed; rejected or not decided within three days, all of it comes back.

What documents are asked for, and who sees them?

For a home or a vehicle, the paper that shows it is yours: a title deed, a lease or the registration. Each file is sealed in your browser to the one party that will decide, opens nowhere else, and is deleted once the application is decided. My cover shows what you sent and who opened it.

It says "Not sent: the company has no reading key yet". What now?

The company that will decide has not yet opened its desk in a browser, so there is no key to seal your documents to. Nothing was sent. The company will ask for its documents another way, or you can add them from My cover once it has a key.

On the demonstration, who approves my application?

You do. Sign in as a company, take the issuing stand in's desk on the Company page and decide it there; or decide it as the operator, under Applications.

For companies

How does a company register and start writing?

Register with the licence details, the LEI, the company's domain and its documents by kind. The operator then works a checklist: the legal entity, control of the domain, the licence and the classes it names, the signatory's authority, sanctions, live enforcement and, in Türkiye, membership of the insurers' association. Nothing is written until every required item is confirmed, and the checks fall due again a year on.

What does the platform take from a company?

One per cent of each premium, out of the company's own fronting fee, the moment the fee is paid, never more than the fee itself. Every movement is on the company's monthly statement, with CSV, and a fee returned to a buyer returns the platform's share too.

What does the company's desk show?

Its book, worked: search by policy number, street, place or buyer; filters by status, province and peril; the policies ending within thirty days; open cover by province; CSV. And the applications waiting for its decision, with the documents sealed to it.

Messages on the page, and what they mean

"The protocol could not be reached, or answered with an error the page could not read. Nothing was charged."

The page asked the protocol and got no readable answer within its patience: a dropped connection, a restart, or a slow reply. Nothing was taken. Reload the page and try again; if it persists, the protocol is down and the Live line at the top will say so.

The price bar stays on "Pricing…"

The protocol has not answered the price request yet. It normally takes a few seconds; a minute or more means it could not be reached. Reload the page; the local figure is only an estimate until the protocol's price replaces it.

"The protocol did not price this" or "The pool will not carry this"

The pool's own rules refused: capital, concentration, or a place or peril it does not write. The reasons are shown in the same box, in the protocol's words.

"The protocol could not read what the page sent:"

A field the protocol refused, named in the message. Almost always an old page talking to a newer protocol: reload the page and go through the steps again.

"Cover of … exceeds the … you declared at risk"

Cover above the value at risk would be a wager rather than an indemnity, so it is refused rather than priced. Lower the cover, or raise the declared value if it was wrong.

"What you are insuring is not inside the area this index measures"

The place you chose is not where the thing stands, or the index is measured elsewhere. Choose the place where the home, the car or the field actually is.

"but not for this peril", or no company offers to issue it

No licensed company writes this peril at this place, or the ones that do have no room. Try another amount or term; on the demonstration the stand ins' licences decide what can be written where.

"The price moved while you were deciding"

The book moved between the price you were shown and the moment you signed, and the new price is higher than what you agreed to. Nothing was taken. Ask for a fresh price and decide again.

"Too many requests"

Each address and each page is limited in how often it may ask for prices or sign in, to keep the protocol up for everyone. Wait a minute and try again.

"This browser does not hold the key these were sealed to"

The applicant sealed the documents to the reading key of the browser that first opened this desk. Open them there, or ask the applicant to send them again from this browser.

"That session ended" on the operator's page

Operator sessions last forty five minutes and are not kept across a restart. Sign in again with the token.

Complaints

Where do I complain?

On the policy itself, under My cover: "Raise a complaint" opens a thread with the issuing company, and its time to answer is counted from that moment (fifteen days in Türkiye). If it does not answer in time, or the answer does not settle it, one click takes it to the ombudsman (the Sigorta Tahkim Komisyonu in Türkiye) with the file its form asks for: the policy, every reading and settlement, the whole thread. The Proof page shows the same readings, so a dispute starts from the facts.

For insurers

Your licence, our balance sheet.

Clause does not sell insurance. It prices risk, holds the capital that stands behind it, and settles automatically from a reading the validators agree on. What it cannot do is issue a policy to a person in your country, because that takes a licence, and a licence is yours.

So a country on the map below is there because a company registered for it. There is no list of countries anywhere in this protocol. Register, have your licence checked, and your country appears the same day.

       "subject":{"route":"TK-IST-LHR","flight_number":"TK1979","departure_date":"2026-11-04"}}'

  -H "x-clause-key: ck_live_…" -H "content-type: application/json" \
  -d '{"peril":"flight_delay","limit":800,"term_days":2,"subject":{…},
       "external_ref":"BOOKING-8812",
       "interest":{"what":"a return trip","country":"TR","inside_trigger_area":true,
                   "value_at_risk":1200,"size":{"kind":"trip","value":1200}},
       "beneficiary":{"payout":{"method":"bank","iban":"TR…","holder_name":"…"}}}'

curl https://api.clause.insure/api/partner/v1/policies?external_ref=BOOKING-8812 -H "x-clause-key: ck_live_…"

    Binding is idempotent on external_ref: sending the same reference again returns the policy already bound, so a retry after a timeout never buys twice.

    Webhooks carry x-clause-timestamp and x-clause-signature, the HMAC SHA-256 of the timestamp, a full stop and the body, keyed with your webhook secret. Events: policy.bound and policy.settled, with the outcome paid, lapsed or expired.
